The state of local sports media

Volunteer-grade photography on a wire-service deadline.

Most Maltese sports photography is volunteer-grade or generic. Files arrive late, lighting is improvised, and the imagery clubs and athletes actually need to win sponsors and grow profile never materialises. Something has to give — and on most match days, it's the picture.

  • Edits filed two or three days after the final whistle — too late for the Monday news cycle.
  • Spray-and-pray shooting that misses the moment a sponsor would actually pay to use.
  • No multi-sport coverage: clubs and federations have to source three photographers for three codes.
  • Licensing left ambiguous, leaving athletes and sponsors unsure what they can actually publish.

The practice · How it works

Match-day, season-long, on-demand — filed on a newsroom clock.

A single working photojournalist running the practice end-to-end. Three coverage tiers, the discipline of newspaper photography, and a turnaround built for the news cycle that follows the final whistle.

01 · Speed

Same-day social, 24-hour gallery.

Match-ready edits posted within hours of the final whistle. Full high-resolution gallery delivered inside 24 hours. Late media is dead media; turnaround is part of the product.
